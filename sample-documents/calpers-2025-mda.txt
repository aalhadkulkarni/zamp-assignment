To the Members of the Board of Administration and to the members and
employers of the System:

I am pleased to present the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report of the
California Public Employees' Retirement System for the fiscal year ended
June 30, 2025. Responsibility for both the accuracy of the data and the
completeness and fairness of the presentation rests with System
management.

The System administers several defined benefit plans. The largest is the
Public Employees' Retirement Fund, reported in three segments — PERF A
covering state members, PERF B covering school members, and PERF C
covering participating public agencies. The Legislators' Retirement
System and the Judges' Retirement System II are administered separately
and are not consolidated into the figures discussed below unless
described as system-wide.

Unless otherwise noted, all dollar amounts in the discussion that
follows are expressed in thousands.

PAGE 19 — MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS: FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The fiscal year was a strong one for the System. Global equity markets
delivered returns well above the long-term expectation, and the private
markets programme continued to mature.

Investments. The System's investment portfolio, carried at fair value,
stood at 462,090,073 for PERF A at the close of the year, against
441,388,105 a year earlier. Taking all plans together, total investments
at fair value were 502,073,818, an increase of 9.4 percent on the prior
year. Roughly 51 percent of the portfolio was held in public equity, with
private equity and real assets accounting for a further 28 percent
between them.

Receivables. Amounts owed to the System at year end fell into three
categories. Employer and member contributions receivable stood at
3,214,556 for PERF A. Investment income receivable, comprising accrued
dividends, interest and distributions not yet settled, was 1,884,201.
Other receivables, principally amounts due from brokers on trades that
had not settled by 30 June, came to 105,361. Across all plans the
equivalent figures were 5,612,003, 3,201,441 and 300,260 respectively.
The System does not present a combined receivables line in this
discussion; readers are referred to the Statement of Fiduciary Net
Position for the consolidated presentation.

Assets. Total assets of PERF A, before deferred outflows of resources,
were 508,215,927 at 30 June 2025. The corresponding figure for all plans
combined was 551,388,204. The increase over the prior year is driven
almost entirely by investment performance rather than by contribution
growth, which was broadly flat.

Liabilities. Total liabilities of PERF A, before deferred inflows of
resources, were 98,831,325. Across all plans, total liabilities stood at
106,927,440. The largest single component in both cases is amounts
payable under securities lending arrangements, which move with market
activity and should not be read as an indicator of obligations to
members.

Net position. After liabilities, net position — restricted for pension,
other post-employment, replacement benefits and program administration —
was 409,384,602 for PERF A. On a system-wide basis, net position
restricted for pensions closed the year at 444,460,764, or approximately
$444.5 billion in whole dollars. This represents an increase of 11.2
percent on the prior year and is the highest year-end figure in the
System's history.

The funded status of the plans is measured annually. As at the valuation
date, the funded ratio on a market value basis was 78.4 percent, up from
72.0 percent in the prior valuation.

The discount rate used to measure the total pension liability was 6.80
percent. This is the rate at which projected benefit payments are
discounted to present value, and it is set by the Board following an
experience study.

Separately, the assumed rate of return on investments used in the
actuarial projections is also 6.80 percent. Although the two rates are
numerically identical in the current valuation, they are distinct
assumptions serving different purposes, and readers should not treat one
as a substitute for the other. The long-term inflation assumption
underlying both is 2.30 percent.

Payroll growth is assumed at 2.80 percent annually. Mortality is
projected using generational tables with a fully generational projection
using Scale MP-2021.

PAGE 57 — INVESTMENT SECTION: POLICY AND GOVERNANCE (EXCERPT)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Board adopts a Total Fund Policy that governs asset allocation,
rebalancing and the use of leverage. The policy is reviewed no less
frequently than every four years, in conjunction with the asset
liability management cycle.

Investment authority is delegated to the Chief Investment Officer within
limits set out in the policy. External managers are retained where the
System does not have, or does not wish to build, an internal capability.
Manager selection follows a documented due diligence process, and all
appointments above a threshold are reported to the Board.

The System's securities lending programme is operated by an appointed
agent. Collateral is held at not less than 102 percent of the market
value of loaned domestic securities and 105 percent for international
securities. Cash collateral is invested in a separately managed
short-term portfolio.

Proxy voting is carried out in accordance with the Governance and
Sustainability Principles, which are published separately and are not
reproduced here.
