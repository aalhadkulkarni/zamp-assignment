PAGE 24 — MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS: OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------

This section presents management's discussion and analysis of the
financial position and performance of the California State Teachers'
Retirement System for the year ended 30 June 2025. It should be read in
conjunction with the financial statements that follow it.

The System administers the State Teachers' Retirement Plan, a
cost-sharing multiple-employer defined benefit plan, together with the
Pension2 defined contribution programme and the Teachers' Health
Benefits Fund. Except where stated otherwise, the discussion below
concerns the Defined Benefit Program of the State Teachers' Retirement
Plan.

All amounts in this section are stated in whole dollars.

PAGE 25 — MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS: FINANCIAL POSITION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Investments. The System's investments, measured at fair value, totalled
331,842,006,118 at year end, compared with 308,554,219,067 a year
earlier. The increase reflects both market appreciation and net
contributions received during the year. The Real Estate and Private
Equity portfolios were the largest contributors in absolute terms.

Receivables. Total receivables at 30 June 2025 were 4,882,110,447. This
comprises member and employer contributions receivable, investment
income accrued but not received, and amounts due from brokers on
unsettled trades. A detailed breakdown is provided in Note 3 to the
financial statements.

Assets. Total assets, before deferred outflows of resources, stood at
349,117,552,904. Cash and cash equivalents represented a little under
one percent of that figure at year end, consistent with the System's
practice of remaining substantially invested.

Liabilities. Total liabilities, before deferred inflows of resources,
were 12,006,338,771. The majority relates to obligations under the
securities lending programme and to trades awaiting settlement at the
reporting date.

Plan net assets. After deducting liabilities, plan net assets held in
trust for pension benefits were 337,111,214,133 at 30 June 2025. On the
prior year's basis this represents growth of 8.1 percent. The System
uses the term plan net assets throughout this report; it is equivalent
to the net position restricted for pensions presented by other
California systems.

PAGE 31 — ACTUARIAL SECTION (EXCERPT)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most recent actuarial valuation of the Defined Benefit Program was
carried out as at 30 June 2024 and rolled forward to the reporting date.

The discount rate applied in measuring the total pension liability was
7.10 percent. The assumed long-term rate of return on plan investments,
net of investment expenses, is 7.10 percent. These assumptions are
reviewed as part of the experience study conducted every four years and
are not interchangeable, notwithstanding that they presently coincide.

The funded ratio on an actuarial value basis was 76.5 percent at the
valuation date.

Wage growth is assumed at 3.50 percent annually and price inflation at
2.75 percent.

PAGE 63 — INVESTMENT SECTION: PORTFOLIO STRUCTURE (EXCERPT)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Investment Policy and Management Plan sets the strategic asset
allocation and the permissible ranges around it. Rebalancing is
undertaken when an asset class moves outside its range, subject to
transaction cost considerations.

The Collaborative Model, adopted by the Board to increase the proportion
of assets managed internally, continued to be implemented during the
year. Management estimates that the initiative has reduced external
management costs materially since inception, and reporting on this is
provided to the Investment Committee semi-annually.

Investments in derivative instruments are permitted for hedging and for
efficient portfolio management, and are not used to create leverage at
the total fund level beyond the limits set out in the policy.

The System reports on its climate-related financial disclosures
separately, in accordance with California statutory requirements.
